**p4n4-dashboard 1.4.0** puts the rest of p4n4-api on screen and looks after more than one site: logs, devices, sensors, the inference model and device commands; a fleet view of every client with thresholds and outage history; and a few things that make the dashboard easier to live with.

```bash
docker pull ghcr.io/raisga/p4n4-dashboard:1.4.0    # amd64 and arm64
```

> **Trusted networks only,** like the rest of p4n4. Keep port 8088 on your LAN. If it must be reachable from elsewhere, serve it over HTTPS (`docker-compose.tls.yml`) with basic auth on.

## Highlights

- **Services in detail.** Admins and power users see each service's version, how long it has run (or why it stopped) and whether its healthcheck fails.
  - Admins follow a stack's logs, or one service's, as they come: filter them, copy them, jump back to the newest line.
  - Admins restart a single service instead of its whole stack.
  - A service that's down says for how long: amber at first, red once it has been down longer than the deployment's threshold.
- **Devices** (Settings → Devices): the devices that send readings to p4n4-api, with when each was last seen.
  - Admins register a device and see its key once, with a copy button; disable it, give it a new key, or remove it.
  - Power users see the list.
- **The Device tab, beyond the board's readings:**
  - **Sensors:** live readings from the devices, one tile per sensor; a tile opens its last hour, day or week.
  - **Model** (admins and power users): the inference runner's backend, model, labels and counters, a form to classify values by hand, and its recent results.
  - **Send a command** (admins and power users): one MQTT message to a topic. A topic the deployment doesn't allow says so, and names the p4n4-api settings that decide it.
- **The fleet** (Clients): every client deployment as a card, with its status, its edge device's processor and temperature over the last quarter hour, and the outages seen from this device ("Node-RED down 14:02–14:09"). The history button shows the last 50.
- **Thresholds** (Settings → Thresholds, per deployment): when processor, memory, disk and temperature readings show as high or critical, on Home, the Device tab and Clients, and how many minutes a service can be down before that's critical. A brand or project can set its own (`thresholds` in its defaults).
- **Home** says how long a service has been down. When readings stop refreshing, Home says how old they are rather than showing them as current.
- **Contact support:** with `support` in brand.json (an email address, a phone number and/or a web page), viewers get a **Contact support** button where they'd otherwise be stuck: on Home's status card when something's down, and when the Assistant or the Device tab can't be reached.
- **The Assistant:**
  - Copy any message. **Try again** asks the last question again after a failure, or for another answer.
  - An answer style per device: brief, step by step or plain words. Admins and power users can also write their own instructions.
- **Light camera grid** (Settings → Appearance): for slow links, a new picture every 2 seconds per camera instead of live motion.
- **Request IDs:** every call to p4n4-api carries an ID, and errors shown to admins and power users end with it, so a report can be matched to the server's log.

## Compatibility

- Works with **p4n4-api 0.3.0**: everything above uses endpoints it already has. Sensors need its MQTT connection and InfluxDB; the Model section needs an inference runner.
- `.env.example` pins `DASHBOARD_VERSION=1.4.0`.

## Upgrading from 1.3.0

- Set `DASHBOARD_VERSION=1.4.0` in the dashboard layer's `.env`. Nothing else changes.
- Readings now turn amber and red at the deployment's thresholds. The defaults are the old fixed levels (75/90 % for processor and memory, 70/80 °C), except disk, which now turns amber at 80 % and red at 95 % (it was 75/90 %).

## Limitations

- Outages are recorded by each device from its own status checks, only while it shows that deployment's status. An outage nobody was looking at isn't in the history.
- On web, the light grid slows snapshot cameras only; MJPEG streams stay live there.
- The answer style goes to Ollama models; a Letta agent keeps its own persona.
- The new features were tested against mocked p4n4-api responses and the demo build, not yet against a live deployment.
- The new labels in languages other than English haven't been reviewed by native speakers.
